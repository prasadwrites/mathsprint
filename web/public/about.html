Why we built it

AI can write a practice set in seconds, but it makes arithmetic slips, and a wrong answer key is the last thing a tutor wants to explain mid-session. So MathSprint doesn't take its word for it: every answer is solved a second time by a separate math engine before a student sees it.

How a deck gets made

  • You type a topic and level, like "related rates, AP Calc AB," or upload a worksheet or chapter PDF.
  • Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, writes the questions and step-by-step solutions.
  • SymPy, an open source math engine, solves each question again on its own. Anything that disagrees is rewritten, and anything that still doesn't check out is left out of the deck.
  • We read an uploaded PDF to build the deck, then don't store the file.

Who it's for, and who it isn't

MathSprint is for high school and college students, from Algebra 1 through AP Calculus and college math, SAT and ACT prep, and subjects like physics and chemistry. It's for the tutors, teachers and parents who set their practice, too. Accounts are for ages 13 and up.

It isn't a full tutoring business tool: there's no scheduling, invoicing or lesson notes. It does practice decks, timed tests and results.
